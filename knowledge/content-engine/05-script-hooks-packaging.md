# 05. Script, hooks and packaging

The binding versions live in `rules/script.md`. This file gathers the sources and the full reasoning.

## Packaging before production (abx.direct, ref 22)
This system took a channel from zero to 2M views on its second video in under two months:
1. **ICP:** who exactly the audience is.
2. **How we talk to them.**
3. **Value:** what they get from the video.
4. **The payoff question:** the question the video promises to answer at the end.
5. **Packaging, built BEFORE filming:** script structure (the core points), titles, thumbnails/cover.
For us: every plan item gets ICP, voice, value and payoff question before any script is written. The November plan
already has persona, hook, CTA and KPI; it lacks the payoff question and the cover.

## The 3 loops (thewolfofmarketingstreet, ref 21)
- **Hook loop (0-5 s):** create curiosity.
- **Intensifier loop (about 5-15 s):** make the curiosity impossible to ignore. Most people skip this one.
- **Rehook loop (mid-video):** renew curiosity in the middle. This is what takes good storytelling to exceptional.
"Great storytellers think about loops more than structure." Close the loops in order.

## Triple hook (kallawaymarketing, ref 24)
1. **Context, immediately.** People watch only what they have a stake in. Say it in the audio, show it
   in the visual, write it on screen; ideally all three. Their data: delaying the context even to the back
   half of the first sentence raises the skip rate a lot.
2. **Lean.** Push curiosity in one direction with proof or a stat, a pain or a wanted benefit, or something
   shocking. Test: read the first 1-2 sentences alone. Is the context clear? Does something pull?
3. **Contrarian snapback.** Pull the viewer the opposite way from where they were leaning ("hook the
   fish one way, yank the line the other"). Kallaway says about 1 % of people do this.

## 25 craft rules from 7 YouTube tutorials (ref 27, research agent)
Full list with sources: `research/2026-11-06-reel-craft-videos.md`. Matched videos:

- heyDominik, "10k followers": https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7F7X7tfJZN0
- Gaku Lange, "5 creative shots": https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rbF4rEEApfM
- dearelaine: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dw1gdIW1YGs
- Natasha Pierre, "20 B-roll shots": https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4mjkb3nDec
- Modern Millie: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IQcPXyH71hY
Not found: the "Oscar" tutorial and the "Instagram reels tutorial".

The ones that change our scripts most:
- Frame 1 gives context and makes the viewer ask ONE specific question. Never "who can relate?".
- The default hook challenges a common belief, in one short line.
- Never reveal the answer in frame 1. Name the tension, hide the giveaway, pay it off at the end.
- Every payoff opens the next question. The reveal comes last, so the reel is watched through.
- Put a person and an emotion in it (then vs now). A list of nice visuals is not a story.
- Say the save/share CTA naturally: «احفظه» or «أرسله لشخص…».
- Visual reels about 15-20 s. Reuse proven formats: belief challenge, before/after, reveal, split-screen, teach-one-thing.

## Applying it to our reels (worked example: Taj, November plan item 1)
- Context (0-1 s): «موقع معرض صور… حجمه أربعة وثلاثين ميقا».
- Lean: «العميل ما ينتظر أكثر من ثلاث ثواني».
- Snapback: «بس المشكلة مو في الاستضافة ولا السيرفر».
- Intensifier: the visitor leaves, the hourglass empties, the competitor's site opens.
- Rehook (middle): «وطلع الحل شي محد يسويه».
- Payoff: 34 MB → 140 KB, the page opens before you blink. Real Taj screen recording.
- CTA: a save or DM action, no hard sell; this item is pillar Proof, objective [F].
Three trial variants change only the first 2-3 s: (a) number first, (b) pain first, (c) contrarian first.

## Gulf script vocabulary (from earlier feedback)
Use: سوّى (not بنى for "made"), وش، محد، يطيح، مو، بيدين. Numbers are written as Arabic words for TTS.
English technical terms stay in English. Site copy is light Gulf; SEO titles and H1s stay MSA (project
memory `feedback-site-gulf-dialect`). The Volume TikTok slideshows were formal MSA, a different channel.
